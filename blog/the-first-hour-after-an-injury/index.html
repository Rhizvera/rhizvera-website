The First Hour After an Injury

The claims procedures a staffing company controls, and why they matter even though the adjuster handles the claim

Rhizvera · November 28, 2026

Who this is for: staffing company owners, branch and on-site staff, and the programs, TPAs and adjusters that work with them.

When a temporary worker is injured, most staffing companies think of the claim as the adjuster’s job. The adjuster investigates, decides compensability, manages medical care and sets reserves. That is right. But the staffing company controls the first hour, and the first hour shapes what the adjuster has to work with.

Two Employers, One Injury

A temporary worker is usually injured at a host employer’s facility, under the host employer’s supervision. The staffing company may hear about it from the worker, from the host’s supervisor, or not at all until the worker calls days later. Every hour of delay makes the facts harder to confirm and the worker harder to reach.

Staffing companies that handle injuries well agree with each host employer, before the first placement, who calls whom, how fast, and what information is passed on.

Confirm the Basics While You Can

At the time of the injury, confirm:

  • How to reach the worker: current phone number, address and preferred language. Temporary workers change numbers and addresses more often than most employees, and a claim with no working contact stalls.
  • Who saw it happen, and who supervised the worker that day.
  • What the worker was doing at the moment of injury.

Check the Job Against the Approval

That last point deserves its own step. Compare what the worker was doing with the job the worker was approved for. If a packer was hurt on a forklift, the claim may be classified differently, the training question will come up, and the staffing company needs to know now that the job changed. The finding also matters for every other worker at that jobsite.

Get the Worker to the Right Care

State rules on medical care differ. In Georgia, for example, the employer posts a panel of physicians and the injured worker chooses from it. In California, a worker who qualified and named a personal physician in writing before the injury may see that doctor. Know the rules for each state where you place workers, and make sure on-site staff and host supervisors know where to send an injured worker. For a serious injury, emergency care comes first and paperwork waits.

File What Each Situation Requires

The forms and notices required depend on the state and on what happened: first aid only, a refusal of treatment, a clinic visit, emergency transport, lost time. Build a short guide for each state and each situation so branch staff do not have to work it out under pressure. Report the claim to the carrier or TPA promptly. State deadlines are short: in Florida, for example, the employer must report an injury to its carrier within 7 days of learning of it.

Know What OSHA Requires

Separate from the claim, OSHA requires employers to report a work-related fatality within 8 hours, and an in-patient hospitalization, amputation or loss of an eye within 24 hours. For OSHA’s injury log, the employer that supervises the temporary worker day to day, usually the host employer, records the injury. OSHA and NIOSH recommend that the host and the staffing company tell each other promptly about any injury either one learns of.

Log Every Contact With the Injured Worker

Every call, text and conversation with the injured worker should be recorded with the date, who spoke and what was said, and shared with the adjuster. A worker who hears nothing may look elsewhere for answers. A worker who hears from someone promptly and respectfully knows the claim is being handled. A record also protects the staffing company if what was said is later disputed.

Plan the Return to Work

A staffing company can sometimes offer transitional work at another customer when the host employer cannot. Raise it with the adjuster early. Modified duty that fits the doctor’s restrictions keeps the worker connected and can shorten the claim.

Procedures, Not Adjusting

None of this replaces the adjuster. It is the staffing company doing its part, so the adjuster starts with accurate facts, a reachable worker and a clear picture of the job. Programs that insure staffing companies should ask to see these procedures in writing, and check that they are followed.
