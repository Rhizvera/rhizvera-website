Who Holds Your Staffing Company’s Risk History?

The records a staffing company should keep in its own hands, and why they matter when it changes carriers, programs, agents or service providers

Rhizvera · November 28, 2026

Who this is for: staffing company owners and CFOs.

A staffing company’s workers’ compensation history is one of its most valuable business records. It supports pricing at renewal, access to new markets, collateral terms and the value of the company if it is ever sold. Yet many staffing companies cannot produce that history without asking someone else for it.

Where the History Lives

The records are usually spread across the carrier, the agent, the TPA, a PEO or staffing EOR, a payroll provider and the company’s own branches. Each holds part of the story. When a relationship ends, the part it held can become slow, costly or impossible to get back.

Records Worth Keeping in Your Own Files

  • Loss runs from every carrier and program, requested regularly, not only at renewal. Markets want them recently valued: the State Compensation Insurance Fund of California, for example, asks for four years of loss runs valued within 100 days of the policy’s start date.
  • Every experience rating worksheet, by bureau and year, so changes in your mod can be checked and explained. An NCCI mod generally uses three years of experience and leaves out the most recent year, so a change often traces back to policy years that ended long ago.
  • Classification decisions for each host employer, jobsite and job, with the duties reviewed and who approved them.
  • Monthly payroll reports, and the reconciliation of reported payroll to Form 941 wages.
  • Final audit reports, any disputes, and how each was resolved.
  • Claims procedure records: injury reports, contact logs with injured workers and return-to-work offers.
  • Policies, endorsements and certificates for every state where workers were placed.

Read the Arrangement Before You Need It

Staffing companies that work through a PEO or a staffing EOR should understand who holds the workers’ compensation history under that arrangement. In some arrangements the coverage and the loss history sit on the provider’s policy. Under an NCCI master policy, for example, the PEO is the first named insured and the experience is reported under the PEO’s mod. The staffing company may have little of its own to show when it leaves. Ask before you sign: what history will we be able to take with us, in what form, and how quickly?

Keep It When Agents Change

When a staffing company changes its agent, the new agent needs the history to market the account, and the outgoing agent keeps the records from the years it handled. A staffing company that holds its own copies depends on neither.

History Is Evidence

An underwriter reviewing a staffing company with steady classification records, reconciled payroll and documented claims procedures has evidence, not just assurances. The same records help at audit, in a classification dispute, and in due diligence if the company is sold.

A Simple Test

Ask your team to produce, within one business day, the last five years of loss runs, mod worksheets and final audits, and the approved classification for every current job. Whatever cannot be produced is history someone else controls.
